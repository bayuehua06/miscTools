# The Impact of Sleep Duration on Working Memory in College Students

Sleep is a fundamental biological process that supports cognitive functioning. Recent studies suggest that insufficient sleep may impair *working memory*, which is essential for academic performance (Walker, 2017).

## Method

### Participants

Participants were undergraduate students enrolled in an introductory psychology course. Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

### Materials

The study used a standardized digit-span task and a sleep diary.

## Results

Students who reported fewer than six hours of sleep performed significantly worse on the digit-span task than those who reported seven or more hours.

Table 1
*Mean Digit-Span Scores by Sleep Group*

| Group | n | M | SD |
|-------|---|---|----|
| Short sleep (< 6 hr) | 42 | 5.21 | 1.10 |
| Adequate sleep (≥ 7 hr) | 48 | 7.04 | 0.98 |

*Note.* Scores are based on the digit-span task administered in Session 2.

## Discussion

These findings are consistent with previous research indicating that sleep restriction reduces cognitive efficiency. Limitations include reliance on self-reported sleep duration.
